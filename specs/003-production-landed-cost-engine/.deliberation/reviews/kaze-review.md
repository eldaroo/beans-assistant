# Kaze review: Production and Landed-Cost Engine

Creative Director of Living Systems. Six answers, in my voice.

## 1. What problem is this actually solving

The CEO is running a business on a number he cannot see. He prices bracelets on a feeling and ships to three countries on faith. What he feels today is a low, constant hum of not-knowing. When the landed number is finally honest and visible, that hum stops. He exhales. He can say "una pulsera en Costa Rica me sale esto" and point at it. The product is not a dashboard. The product is that exhale. Honesty made legible. He stops guessing and starts deciding.

## 2. The smallest first version that proves the idea

One screen. One bracelet type, one country, one honest landed number in USD. The BOM lines stacked as a column that sums down to the total, each line showing its value or its gap. The total is not a hero figure floating alone; it sits at the bottom of its own reasoning, so the eye reads the components first and arrives at the sum last. That screen breathes before anything else is built. If that one card feels true, the rest is extension. If it lies, nothing downstream matters.

## 3. Three risks that would kill this

First, the false-precise total. A number rendered confident while a shipping or customs line is empty is a betrayal dressed as competence. The total must carry its own doubt. It shows "parcial" with the count of filled versus pending components, never a clean figure pretending to be whole.

Second, density that drowns orientation. If the CEO opens the home and cannot answer "where am I, what do I do" inside five seconds, the platform is dead no matter how correct the math. One primary number, one next action, silence around them.

Third, the supplier surface that feels like surveillance. María and Víctor logging their own costs must feel like contribution, not a camera pointed at them. The moment it reads as monitoring, they stop entering honest data, and the whole engine starves.

## 4. Success at 90 days

The CEO opens the platform first thing, not the spreadsheet. He sets a price off the screen without a calculator. María and Víctor enter their own numbers without being chased, because their surface is calm and theirs. At least one real "where do I produce" decision was made by comparing countries on screen. The old sales assistant is still there, quieter, and nobody mourns it.

## 5. Atomic tasks from a creative-direction angle

1. The primary landed-cost screen: the component column that sums to one USD number per type per country.
2. The completeness and PENDING treatment: how a gap looks, how "parcial" reads, how a filled line settles differently from an empty one.
3. The supplier self-entry surface: María and Víctor see only their own inputs, framed as their contribution, never margins or peers.
4. The nav reframe: costing becomes home, sales and stock and expenses recede to secondary without being torn out.
5. The country comparison view: Colombia, Armenia-Colombia, Costa Rica side by side, the cheapest one honestly marked.
6. The empty and first-load states: a new type with nothing filled must feel like an invitation to complete, not a broken form.

## 6. The one thing only my faculty would notice

The suppliers and the CEO must never share one screen wearing two masks. Build them as two different rooms with two different temperatures from the first commit. One surface forced to hide the CEO's margins with conditionals will leak, visually or morally, and the leak is the exact consent harm this spec already fears. Separate rooms, not one room with locked drawers.
