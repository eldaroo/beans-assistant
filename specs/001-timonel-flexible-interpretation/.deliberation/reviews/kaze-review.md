# Kaze review — Timonel flexible interpretation

Faculty: the felt experience of the interaction. The UI here is the words. These lines are the product.

## 1. What problem is this actually solving

A shop owner spoke like a person and the helmsman answered like a form. She said "medias azules y grises a 5 dolares" the way she would tell an employee, and got back "ese dato, ese dato". The real problem is not parsing. It is trust on the first day. The empty catalog is the most fragile moment we have: she is deciding whether this thing is worth her time. One machine apology and she closes the tab. We are solving for the felt sense that someone competent is on the other side.

## 2. The smallest first version that proves the idea

One path, end to end, with the right words: "medias azules y grises a 5 dolares" expands to two products and Timonel confirms what it did in plain language. If that single turn feels like talking to a capable assistant instead of submitting a form, the idea is proven. Everything else is range.

## 3. The 3 risks that would kill this

- Fixing the parser and leaving the copy robotic. She does not see `items[0].name`. She sees how it talks to her. Correct extraction with cold copy still loses her.
- Over-confirming. If Timonel narrates every micro-step, the chat becomes noise and she stops reading. Confirm the outcome, not the machinery.
- A failure path that blames her. "Revisá la lista y volvé a intentar" makes a system bug her homework. That line must die.

## 4. What success looks like at 90 days

Owners load their whole catalog in one sitting, in their own words, and never once feel they are filling a form. The word "fallida" never appears for a thing that half-worked. Returning owners type loose, trust the assistant to expand it, and are right to.

## 5. The copy contract (acceptance copy the implementation must match)

Voseo. No exclamation marks, no emojis, no hype. These exact lines:

(a) After expanding "medias azules y grises a 5 dolares" into two:
> Listo, cargué dos productos a 5 dólares: Medias Azules y Medias Grises. Seguimos con el resto?

(b) After "medias multicolor talle s, m y l a 10 dolares" into three:
> Cargué tres: Medias Multicolor talle S, talle M y talle L, todas a 10 dólares. Va el siguiente?

(c) Honest partial success, one variant collides:
> Cargué Medias Multicolor talle S y talle M a 10 dólares. La talle L no entró porque ya tenés una igual cargada. Querés que la saltee o la actualizo?

(d) Genuine missing data, naming the product and the field:
> De las medias azules tengo el color pero me falta el precio. A cuánto las vendés?

What makes each line a shopkeeper's assistant and not a validator: it says what it DID before what it needs (a, b). It owns the gap without alarm and offers a real next move (c). It names the actual product and the one real field in her words, never "ese dato" (d). It closes by handing the turn back to her plainly, so the conversation keeps breathing.

## 6. The one cue only my faculty caught

The rhythm of the apology. Today every reply opens on the failure: "No pude cargar nada", "Operación fallida". The first three words set the emotional register of the whole turn, and right now they all open on defeat. Even the partial success that landed two of three products reads as a loss because the framing leads with what broke. Reorder it. Lead with what landed, then name the one gap calmly, then hand her the next move. Same facts, opposite feeling. The fix is not only in the parser. It is in which clause comes first.
